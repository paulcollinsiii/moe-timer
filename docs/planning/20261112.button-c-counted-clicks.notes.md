## Design decisions (and why)

- **The gesture opens on the EXT1 wake press and on a C latched during a tick
  wake** (`wake_flow_counted_swap`, called from `wake_flow_handle_button_wake`'s
  `case BTN_C` and from `wake_flow_handle_timer_tick`'s latch drain, which is
  also where a C pressed in the grid wait or the sync lands). Presses made
  before the drain are one latch bit and count once. The break tail's poll keeps
  a single swap through `wake_flow_dispatch_button_action`: it ends its watch on
  the first applied press by design. (The first pass scoped this to the EXT1
  press only; the plan says "the wake press, or a latched one".)
- **State is two locals and a counter.** The candidate is walked with the pure
  `timer_slot_after`; nothing is written until `timer_select_slot(candidate)`
  at the end, which refuses a lap that lands on the active slot (so "home" =
  nothing written). No `rtc_state_t` change.
- **Loop** mirrors the chore coalescer: sliding quiet window, hard budget, step
  clamped to the nearer, so neither overshoots. Poll is 20 ms (the chore one is
  50): the poll is the release gate's resolution, and a press made before the
  sample that sees the release is lost.
- **Paint.** Commit goes through the unchanged render decision
  (`render_action_result` with `selection_changed = true`), so the three
  promotions apply unchanged and nothing forces a full (decision 9). A lap home
  skips the first render only (`finish_or_break_painting(..., paint=false)`):
  the snapshot, join and owed-window steps still run. The skip is refused by
  `wake_policy_swap_paint_owed` when a break ended this wake or the day rolled
  over (`s_day_rolled`, new, set in `wake_flow_handle_day_rollover`) — each of
  which the swap's paint would otherwise have carried — and only on the EXT1
  path: a tick wake's minute render has already painted both (`glass_current`).
  There is no lock-screen term: a wake whose lock gate let go takes no press.
- **Refused C (active timer RUNNING, or no extras)** keeps today's behaviour:
  no strip, no wait, one repaint.
- **Per-button window.** Two arrays in the latch: the configured window (what the
  next accepted press opens) and the stamped window (what the last accepted
  press opened, which `record` compares against). Stamping means a change never
  reaches into a running window, and a SEEDED press always stamps the default:
  the wake press anchors at reset, a boot after the press, and nothing knows
  when its release rings.
- **When the mode is known.** The latch is armed in `buttons_init` before any
  wake handler, so every window is the default then. The short window is
  installed by the gesture when it OPENS (after rollover, bed-time and the
  break-end drain, so the mode is final) and put back before the commit, on
  every exit. So it is scoped to the gesture: Chores mode, every other wake and
  the rest of this wake keep 300 ms. A mode toggle cannot happen inside the
  gesture (A is not acted on). Wake press A then C: C is not a swap (no ack has
  landed, so the checklist coalescer does not take it either) and the window is
  never touched (test_swap11).
- **The 120 ms window does not cover an ordinary tap's release; the settle does.**
  The release gate and the settle run first at any window. For a press the
  latch ACCEPTED (not the seeded wake press), a hold between 120 ms and 300 ms
  has left the 120 ms window behind when the pad is released (the old 300 ms
  window still covered it), so its release bounce is judged by the gate and the
  settle alone, and a 10 ms settle is shorter than the ringing: a poll sampling
  a high phase, then a bounce edge more than 10 ms later, counted twice (the
  reviewer's simulation: 2 of 20 poll phases at 15 ms of bounce, 7 of 20 at
  25 ms). The settle is therefore per button too
  (`button_latch_set_settle`, stamped at acceptance like the window), and the
  gesture installs `BUTTON_LATCH_SWAP_SETTLE_US` (30 ms) with the 120 ms window
  and puts both back together. The seeded wake press stamps the defaults, so
  its release is covered by its 300 ms window for a hold up to about 300 ms
  after arming and by the 10 ms settle beyond that. Price: the shortest
  release-to-press gap that still counts is poll 20 ms + settle 30 ms. Both are
  starting figures for the bench.
- **Count display is HIGHPRI** (quiet hours do not mute it), like the chore strip.
- **Colour: magenta (25,0,25).** Existing: timer states green (0,20,0), amber,
  red (25,0,0), cyan-blue (0,10,25), white; chore green/red; countdown light
  green (20,60,20); sync pixel blue (0,0,20), green, red (30,0,0).
- **Mapping reuse.** `neopixel_status_binary4` is STATUS class (muted in quiet
  hours) and not host-testable. Its shift is moved unchanged into
  `neopixel_binary4_pixel_lit()` (neopixel.h); `neopixel.c`'s NP_MSG_BINARY4 and
  `status_led_swap_count_for` both call it: one shift in the tree.
- **Strip release** on every exit of an opened gesture: `neopixel_stop()` (wipe,
  gate HIGH) then `wake_flow_show_status_leds()` (state pixel). It is the same
  wipe the countdown does. Enforced by `scripts/check-status-led-wrapper.py`.
- **Other buttons mid-gesture**: the take is masked to C, so B/D/A stay latched
  for the whole gesture (test_swap14 reads the latch at the commit), neither end
  nor join it nor stretch its window; then the handler's existing stale-edge
  drain (`buttons_take_pressed()` just below the dispatch) drops them, as it
  does a B made during any other Timers-mode button wake. A B made mid-gesture
  therefore does nothing.
- **Competing claimants**: none can run inside the gesture (see status_led.h,
  sixth claimant). The gesture needs the active timer not RUNNING, which (S2)
  means no timer is running at all, so no countdown or expiry; no network window
  opens inside it; a Screen Break ending inside it is drained by the commit's
  paint or, on a lap home that paints nothing, by `maybe_wait_for_event`'s
  guaranteed drain, either way after the strip is released.
- **Kconfig host fallbacks.** The brief asked for host fallbacks; the chore knobs
  deliberately have none (a renamed symbol must break the firmware build) and
  `test/CMakeLists.txt` supplies non-default values (410 / 1750). The swap knobs
  follow the chore pattern. Deviation from the brief, deliberate.

## Worst-case arithmetic (SWAP_COUNT_WORST_*)

Kconfig defaults (700 / 6000; the test build's swap knobs are 410 / 1750, so it
exercises the arithmetic but not these figures). One event window W = 42.5 s,
one full paint P = 4 s.

- Gesture's own cost = budget + max(2P, break alarm + P) + W = 6000 +
  max(8000, 7600 + 4000 = 11600) + 42500 = 60.1 s, held under HALF the failsafe
  (90 s). The terms: the waiting (the last quiet window is inside the budget
  because the step is clamped); the commit's paint (the final one) and the
  join's repaint, or on the break-start path the break alarm and its one paint;
  the one window the tail serves. No chime term: a swap plays no sound.
- EXT1 wake press = rollover's window W + gesture = 42.5 + 60.1 = 102.6 s,
  under the WHOLE failsafe (180 s). (The first pass's comment gave 60.1 s
  against 90 s and omitted the rollover; the reviewer's 102 s was right.)
- Latched C on a tick wake = rollover W + sync W + grid wait 25 s + the minute's
  paint P + gesture = 42.5 + 42.5 + 25 + 4 + 60.1 = 174.1 s, under 180 s.
  At the budget's Kconfig maximum (20 s) this asserts out (188.1 s): the build
  refuses a budget over about 11.9 s at default alarms.
- Not charged, and said so in the comment: the follow-up window of an owed-window
  chain (admitted by `wake_flow_headroom_for_window` against the failsafe
  itself), an expiry alert or second owed window ahead of the drain on a tick
  wake (the final-minute assert's territory; a tick wake that stacks every
  worst case is over the failsafe with or without a gesture, which adds only
  its waiting), an OTA check the rollover arms, and the event watch after.

## Press-to-paint, single press (for the owner)

Before: the paint followed the dispatch with no wait (0 ms between the press
being handled and the commit; `test_a_swap_lights_nothing_opens_nothing_and_leaves_the_clock_alone`
still pins that for latched presses). After: one quiet window, 700 ms by default
(pinned: `test_swap1`, commit at exactly the window). Add the usual wake + panel
time to both. The LEDs answer at once in both.

## Surprises / plan discrepancies

- The plan puts the C arm at `wake_flow.c` ~1865; it was ~1846. Equivalent.
- `test_wake_flow` stubs the timer module, so the new timer functions have a
  small faithful model there; the real ones are tested in `test_timer`.
- The existing `test_a_wake_press_is_dispatched_through_the_shared_guard_matrix`
  asserted `timer_select_next` was called once for a C wake; it now asserts the
  gesture's `timer_select_slot` instead (the dispatch arm itself is unchanged and
  still pinned by the `flow_dispatch` cases).
- Taps made while the chip is still booting (before `buttons_init` arms the
  ISRs) are lost, and the seeded wake press's 300 ms window runs from arming, so a
  second tap in the first 300 ms after arming is also refused. How many taps that
  costs depends on wake latency (stream A). If the bench shows the second tap being
  eaten, seeding the short window when the mode is known at arming is the lever
  (one line in `button_latch_seed_press`, plus the order dependency noted above).
